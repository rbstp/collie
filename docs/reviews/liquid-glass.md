# Liquid Glass audit

Issue #207 uses the iOS 26 and watchOS 26 glass APIs directly, with minimum versions at 26. System navigation, toolbars, sheets, menus, pickers and search keep native styling. Prioritize dark mode, keeping native appearance and accessibility; glass uses the system material and respects the user's glass setting.

The app caps Dynamic Type at AccessibilityXL (`.dynamicTypeSize(...DynamicTypeSize.accessibility3)` at the root): every screen is checked to fit at that size on the iPhone 18 Pro Max, portrait and landscape, and the two larger accessibility sizes are not supported.

- **Agent inbox:** segmented filters in a top inset the rows scroll under, across the full width, so all five fit on the iPhone 18 Pro Max and scroll only on narrower phones or with larger text. Search is a button in the toolbar capsule beside Layout and New task; it replaces the filters with the search field on its own glass and a close button, and leaves the capsule while the field is open, so one control ends search. The field's placeholder is in the metadata gray, as in New task (4.97:1 in light mode, 7.4:1 in dark; the system's placeholder gray measured 1.74:1 in light mode). A filter shows its one group without a section header, as the selected filter already names it, the first row 24 points under the filters. The filters and the search field hold still: pulling the rows or the empty state refreshes, with the spinner under them, and a drag on the filter row moves nothing. Each Agents page takes its own refresh, the inbox's on its list alone; on the pager it also reached the filter row's horizontal scroll view, whose refresh control let a drag beside the filters pull the row down out of sight. Rows, state badges and unread indicators stay content.
- **Agent grid:** cards stay content, as terminal previews need a stable background. ## Controls

- **Glass placement.** Glass covers the composer, terminal keys, jump and notice actions, upload and dictation controls, attachment removal and the unlock action. Attachments, dictation, send, keyboard dismissal and attachment removal use native glass button styles. The key strip's main keys (ctrl, shift, esc, tab, delete, return) share one 44-point interactive glass capsule over the dimming color, as in Ghostty's key bar: plain buttons in equal cells with hairline dividers, no glass per key. Labels are 22-point SF Symbols at any text size, as in Rex's key bar: `control`, `shift`, `escape`, `arrow.right.to.line`, `delete.left` and `return`. Large glyphs read at a glance where the earlier callout words (ctrl, shift, esc, tab) looked small in 44-point cells, and fixed sizes keep six cells in the row at accessibility sizes. A selected modifier takes the accent, on a faint accent fill so it stays visible with the None accent. The four arrow keys are gone: the cursor joystick, a 44-point interactive glass circle that is also its touch target, ends the capsule's row, with a primary d-pad glyph at rest and an accent sector toward the arrow it sends while dragged. Combinations, saved shortcuts and **+** sit in a second, scrolling row of 30-point glass capsules, the same shape as the main keys' capsule and the round controls. Combinations show 20-point symbols, as large as the 30-point capsule allows, a modifier's first (`shift` then `arrow.right.to.line`; `control` then the letter, which has no symbol), as does a saved key shortcut without a name; a name or text stays in callout medium, and only a saved shortcut that shows the text it types is monospaced. Each chip's touch target is 44 points tall, reaching into the gaps above and below it, which the row's spacing gives up so the strip keeps its height. Chips with a name or text grow with text size up to xxxLarge. shift-left shows only in Codex sessions, the one agent that takes it, rather than sitting dimmed elsewhere. Adjacent custom glass controls share `GlassEffectContainer`; nothing inside a glass composer or dictation capsule adds more glass.
- **Accent buttons.** Prominent buttons in the accent (Start, send, Jump to bottom, notice options, Unlock, Send feedback, Sign in) take a black label when the accent fill is light, where white falls under 3:1: None and Dark Gold in dark mode (white measured 2.5:1 on Dark Gold), orange and green in both appearances. Blue, red and the light Dark Gold keep white. Disabled, they keep the system's label, as the fill turns gray (black on it read 1.9:1 in dark mode).
- **Warning orange.** Orange notice text and glyphs (the session's link error and blocked hint, Chat's load error, an unreachable machine, a machine's version mismatch, a failed close and a failed move on Agents, the reason's shield) share one color: system orange in dark mode; in light mode, where system orange read 2.3:1 on light backgrounds and even its high-contrast variant (197,83,0) 4.33:1 on the blocked hint's glass, a deeper orange of the same hue (180,75,0); the context and usage limits' orange is the same.
- **Status pills and copy buttons.** App status pills (connection, approval count) and the connection error and prompt copy buttons use regular glass without a dark fill; the approval countdown puts the dimming color behind its glass, as Review does (below). On the approval band and machine rows, they and the band's decision buttons sit glass on glass.
- **Attachment button.** Its native menu stays stable while open and presents Saved prompts, Files or Photo Library after it dismisses. Its 17-point paperclip (see the Terminal composer above) uses the primary color, like the keys, idle dictation and hide-keyboard glyphs: in the composer the accent marks only send, the keys toggle when on and active dictation. Its glass circle matches the 38-point dictation, keyboard and send circles; its 44-point hit target extends past it, as theirs do. Glyphs in these circles, send included, have fixed point sizes, so they stay inside their circles at any text size. Pickers open locally, not waiting for the machine; the upload chip distinguishes preparing, waiting for the machine and uploading.
- **Wrap lines.** A toolbar button, not a button-style toggle, on the shared glass capsule; its icon is accent when on and primary when off, like other glass toolbar icons, and its title carries the state (Wrap lines on or off), as bar buttons pass only their title to accessibility.
- **Segmented controls** (Agents/Usage, inbox filters, Usage costs range, Chat/Terminal). Native, with semibold labels, accent-colored selected text and the system's sliding glass selection, never a forced black or white segment. In dark mode a picker in content (inbox filters, Usage costs range, Chat/Terminal) draws a lighter gray selection lens, where the gold label measured 2.2:1, so its selected label is white instead (6.9:1 on the filters, 5.5:1 on the range, and Chat/Terminal's icons 5.2:1 over Chat and 4.1:1 over the terminal); Agents/Usage in the toolbar keeps a dark lens and the accent (gold 8.6:1), as does every picker in light mode (gold 6.1:1). UIKit leaves a tapped toolbar control's selected label in its pressed tint, so once the press lens settles the control is re-added to its window, restoring the accent and keeping its frame. Dropping UIKit's one-point bottom alignment inset, which the controls draw over, makes Agents/Usage match the capsule beside it in height.
- **Chat/Terminal.** Compact and icon-only (speech bubble, terminal symbol, one SF Symbol language) at the right of the session header, with 44-point segments and VoiceOver labels Chat and Terminal. No glass backing: extra glass beside other glass left keyboard animations unfinished after a picker closed.
- **Agents/Usage paging.** Native interactive page scrolling reveals Usage progressively during a drag rather than switching on release; selector-triggered transitions respect Reduce Motion.
- **Dictation waveform.** An accent-colored translucent gradient, solid with Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast, fills the capsule width with recent microphone levels (silence and repeats included) on a more sensitive scale; its history clears when dictation stops or switches language.

Reference: [Apple's Liquid Glass guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views).
