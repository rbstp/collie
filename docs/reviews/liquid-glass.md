# Liquid Glass audit

Issue #207 uses the iOS 26 and watchOS 26 glass APIs directly, with minimum versions at 26. System navigation, toolbars, sheets, menus, pickers and search keep native styling. Prioritize dark mode, keeping native appearance and accessibility; glass uses the system material and respects the user's glass setting.

The app caps Dynamic Type at AccessibilityXL (`.dynamicTypeSize(...DynamicTypeSize.accessibility3)` at the root): every screen is checked to fit at that size on the iPhone 18 Pro Max, portrait and landscape, and the two larger accessibility sizes are not supported.

- **Agent inbox:** segmented filters, then the search field on its own glass, in a top inset the rows scroll under. Rows, state badges and unread indicators stay content.
- **Agent grid:** cards stay content, as terminal previews need a stable background; context menus use system styling. A preview's running subagents overlay is clear glass over the composer's dark dimming color, always dark, with white text.
- **Every Agents page** (grid, list, inbox, Usage): navigation and tab bars keep system glass with no scroll edge effect or glass behind them, leaving only the picker, buttons and tab bar, each on its own glass. Content runs under the tab bar at full clarity; under the top bar it fades into the page background across the status bar band, because the native top edge effect follows the pager, not each page's own scroll view, and left content readable behind the clock.
- **Approvals:** glass on decision buttons, green for Approve and a red label for Deny; commands, notes and question choices stay content.
- **Conversation:** glass on search, copy and navigation actions and on the activity and wake rows, never accent: tool names, wake lines and Copy are primary, icons, call counts and chevrons in the metadata gray (secondary gray measured 2.9:1 on this glass over a light chat); messages and code stay content, code blocks on the same tertiary fill as your message bubbles, in subheadline monospaced, as are inline code and the file paths in it (at body size, and even a step down at callout, its larger x-height outsized the prose), so code never outsizes body text. A code block's Copy turns into a green check mark (Copied) for three seconds, like the connection error's copy button. Your messages sit in neutral fill bubbles that hug short text at the trailing edge. A conversation shorter than the screen starts under the header, the same gap above it as a longer one has, rather than sitting low above the composer.
- **Git changes / diff review:** glass from #205, unchanged.
- **New task:** the prompt field is plain form content, with glass only on the attachment, upload, dictation and hide-keyboard controls; folder choices and task settings stay form content.
- **Saved prompts / editor:** plain row buttons (no blue tint dimming prompt text) and larger previews; rows and editor stay content.
- **Plan usage:** content; bars, figures and reset times need stable backgrounds.
- **Machines / machine settings / pairing:** system forms; machine details stay content. A machine's link status is plain text with its symbol, not a glass pill inside the row; on Agents, a connected machine shows only a green dot, and other link states keep their pill.
- **QR scanner:** system scanner UI and a native Cancel toolbar item.
- **Settings / gestures:** system switches and a selectable accent color; diagnostic values stay list content.
- **Onboarding:** glass on sign-in and auth-key actions; instructions and input stay form content.
- **Watch app and widgets:** native `List`, `NavigationLink` and `Gauge` throughout, none needing replacement; agent status, commands and approval status stay content. Approve uses `.glassProminent`, Deny and question actions `.glass`.
- **Watch usage detail:** compact native list of logo gauges, window labels, percentages and reset countdowns; tapping a window shows its countdown, reading age and elapsed percentage; no glass on content.
- **Watch complications / Smart Stack:** the rectangular widget shows three native `.accessoryCircular` gauges, provider inside and bold reset countdown below; no window labels, for gauge space (the app shows percentages after a tap). A value-based tint dims the unused arc but keeps the native usage marker; the circular complication keeps elapsed-time ticks. Preserve stale and missing-data states. Gauges use `widgetAccentable` and no extra glass.
- **Live Activity / Dynamic Island:** system-hosted; no forced black background, white system actions or dark appearance on the Lock Screen. Status pills stay content; approval buttons keep the native widget style.

## Agent session

- The bold inline title wraps to two lines, cut without an ellipsis. The bar caps it at 19 points; at accessibility sizes in portrait, where that left it smaller than the status row under it, it leaves the bar and leads the header instead, above the status row, at title 3 size growing with the text (subheadline and headline came out about as large as the status row's caption). While approval cards show it stays in the bar: in the header its lines left the command and the reason no room above the decisions.
- The header (under the navigation bar, with the subagent list) is full-width, edge-to-edge regular glass, with content running under it and no scroll edge effect under the navigation bar, so it reads as one glass area. Regular, not clear: clear glass over a dimming layer still let busy terminal text read through the title, status bar and controls; regular glass blurs it. Its bottom feathers out over 8 points at the default text size, growing with the text (a fixed 8 points cut large text running under it in half), instead of ending on a hard cut, and the glass rim, which drew a hairline seam there, lies past the mask. In light mode a faint gray tint (4% black) over the glass gives it a visible lower edge: untinted, light glass over a white chat measured #FCFCFC, so chat text seemed to run straight into Deny.
- The status row leads with the state pill and elapsed time, then the machine name in secondary caption, which truncates first. The pull request badge, provider, context and the Chat/Terminal switch group on the trailing side. Header text scales with Dynamic Type up to the app's cap: at accessibility sizes in portrait the row splits in two, the state, time and machine above the badge, provider and context, with the switch at the trailing edge, rather than pushing the screen past both edges; landscape keeps one row, as three left the session two lines of the screen. All other header text, subagent time and tokens included, uses the primary color.
- Over the always-dark terminal, the header, composer controls and navigation bar are dark (header and navigation bar only while the terminal runs under them).
- The composer has no band; content shows around its glass controls. Its notice, error, retry and dictation problem lines each get their own regular glass, and each such control and line has a dimming color behind its glass (70% black in dark mode, 80% white in light), in its own shape (small glass alone left busy content hard to read in dark mode).
- Attachment content sits outside the glass composer; the lock notice stays content.
- In the Terminal view the composer has no prompt field, as the keyboard types into the terminal: one row of 38-point glass circles over the dimming color, paperclip and keys on the left, dictation and the keyboard toggle on the right, under the key strip.

## In-session approvals

- The cards form a full-width, edge-to-edge band at the bottom of the session header, sharing its one regular glass (two edge-to-edge glass layers show a seam). Like the header, it is dark over the terminal, following the appearance over Chat.
- Text follows Dynamic Type up to the app's cap, in compact footnote and caption styles with 36-point decision buttons, as in the approved mockup; at accessibility sizes the card scrolls inside its share of the screen. Decision labels never shrink: when one row would cut a label, Approve takes a row of its own above Always allow and Deny, and when two rows would, all three stack; each is judged at the equal widths a row gives its buttons, with 8 points of room on each side of the widest label. Approve's label is semibold, on prominent glass in a deeper green than the system's, so its white label keeps 4.5:1 in both appearances; the equal widths are measured at semibold. Always allow (shorter than the former Approve always, which nearly filled its third of the row) is neutral glass with a regular primary label, Deny glass with a regular label in the Blocked red, the deeper red in light mode, as system red falls under 4.5:1 there.
- A card's header is the tool's name (Bash) and the countdown; the session header above already names the agent and machine. The command sits in its own nested panel in primary footnote monospaced text, readable at rest without scrolling: it wraps at spaces only, never after a hyphen, so a flag such as `--nocapture` stays whole, and a bare `--` stays with the argument after it. Collapsed, it shows up to three lines and, when that cuts it, a Show full command button (caption, secondary rather than accent, so it does not outweigh the command; Full command where the longer label would take two lines) that expands the card; VoiceOver and the expanded card read the whole command. The output under it is secondary: Show full command and the output use the primary color at 60%, 5.3:1 on their panels in light mode and 5.5:1 in dark, where secondary gray and even the metadata gray (4.2:1) fell short over the band's light tint; collapsed, the output shows its first three lines, one line each, and a count of the rest (+5 lines); the chevron expands the command and the output together.
- Nested panels (command, output, reason, question choices) use a 12-point radius.
- While cards show, the composer drops its blocked hint, which the cards already answer, and Chat drops Continue in Terminal unless every card must be answered in the terminal.
- Known limitation: in Chat, VoiceOver touch exploration under the header and the band lands on the conversation, so VoiceOver reaches Approve, Deny and the header's controls only by swiping.
- The cards size to their content up to under half the whole screen's height, keyboard or not (45%, so a command and its output read without scrolling; 55% at accessibility sizes, where 45% left the stacked decisions no room for a line of the reason; a quarter in landscape, so the prompt fits above Approve), and never more than the session header and the composer leave them, since a card fitted to more showed its reason cut under the decisions. With the keyboard up, they give way to what is left above the composer.
- The prompt scrolls inside its card above pinned Approve and Deny; a prompt cut at the card's edge fades to read as scrollable, over 16 points at the default text size and more with larger text, up to 24 (more covered the reason's one line at the largest size), at the bottom and, once scrolled, at the top. If the card would leave the prompt under two command lines (tight landscape, keyboard up), Approve and Deny scroll with it. Collapsed above pinned Approve and Deny, the card shows at rest only what fits whole, so nothing peeks out as a sliver under the fade, in this order: the tool's name; the command, in as many of its three lines as leave Show full command and one line of the reason in view (one at accessibility sizes); the reason, cut to the lines that fit; Add note; the output. With larger text the output goes first, then Add note, then, when not even a line of it fits, the reason (landscape); expanding the card shows them, and the reason whole. When the tool's name and the command outgrow the card's share, it grows past it, so neither sits cut under the decisions. When not even one command line fits with Show full command and a line of the reason, as at the largest accessibility size, the command keeps one line above pinned decisions, then Show full command and the reason, and the prompt scrolls; when the decisions scroll too, as in landscape, it keeps three.
- The countdown pill is in the metadata gray (4.8:1 on the light band), then blocked red in its last minute; the agent's reason sits under the command with a shield symbol, in one treatment in both appearances: a faint orange fill (10%, 6% in dark mode, where 10% still read brown) inside an orange rim (40%), as orange fill alone read as muddy brown over dark glass, and a fill-only panel in light mode looked like a different component.

## Controls

- **Glass placement.** Glass covers the composer, terminal keys, jump and notice actions, upload and dictation controls, attachment removal and the unlock action. Attachments, dictation, send, keyboard dismissal and attachment removal use native glass button styles; approval disclosure is a native button. The key strip's main keys (ctrl, shift, esc, tab, delete, return) share one 44-point interactive glass capsule over the dimming color, as in Ghostty's key bar: plain buttons in equal cells with hairline dividers, no glass per key. Labels are words where every cell fits the widest word at an equal share, which holds at iPhone 17 width up to xLarge; larger, ctrl, shift and tab fall back to the `control`, `shift` and `arrow.right.to.line` symbols rather than shrink or cut. Escape always reads **esc**, not a letter that looks like a character to type; delete and return are the larger `delete.left` and `return` symbols. A selected modifier takes the accent, on a faint accent fill so it stays visible with the None accent. The four arrow keys are gone: the cursor joystick, a 44-point interactive glass circle that is also its touch target, ends the capsule's row, with a primary d-pad glyph at rest and an accent sector toward the arrow it sends while dragged. Combinations, saved shortcuts and **+** sit in a second, scrolling row of 30-point glass capsules (footnote monospaced), the same shape as the main keys' capsule and the round controls. The strip grows with text size up to xxxLarge. ⇧← shows only in Codex sessions, the one agent that takes it, rather than sitting dimmed elsewhere. Adjacent custom glass controls share `GlassEffectContainer`; nothing inside a glass composer or dictation capsule adds more glass.
- **Accent buttons.** Prominent buttons in the accent (Start, send, Jump to bottom, notice options, Unlock, Send feedback, Sign in) take a black label when the accent fill is light, where white falls under 3:1: None and Dark Gold in dark mode (white measured 2.5:1 on Dark Gold), orange and green in both appearances. Blue, red and the light Dark Gold keep white.
- **Status pills and copy buttons.** App status pills (connection, session header, approval countdown) and the connection error and prompt copy buttons use regular glass without a dark fill. On the header, approval band and machine rows, they and the band's decision buttons sit glass on glass.
- **Attachment button.** Its native menu stays stable while open and presents Saved prompts, Files or Photo Library after it dismisses. Its 16-point paperclip uses the primary color, like the keys, idle dictation and hide-keyboard glyphs: in the composer the accent marks only send, the keys toggle when on and active dictation. Its glass circle matches the 38-point dictation, keyboard and send circles; its 44-point hit target extends past it, as theirs do. Glyphs in these circles, send included, have fixed point sizes, so they stay inside their circles at any text size. Pickers open locally, not waiting for the machine; the upload chip distinguishes preparing, waiting for the machine and uploading.
- **Wrap lines.** A toolbar button, not a button-style toggle, on the shared glass capsule; its icon is accent when on and primary when off, like other glass toolbar icons, and its title carries the state (Wrap lines on or off), as bar buttons pass only their title to accessibility.
- **Segmented controls** (Agents/Usage, inbox filters, Usage costs range, Chat/Terminal). Native, with semibold labels, accent-colored selected text and the system's sliding glass selection, never a forced black or white segment. In dark mode a picker in content (inbox filters, Usage costs range, Chat/Terminal) draws a lighter gray selection lens, where the gold label measured 2.2:1, so its selected label is white instead (6.9:1 on the filters, 5.5:1 on the range, and Chat/Terminal's icons 5.2:1 over Chat and 4.1:1 over the terminal); Agents/Usage in the toolbar keeps a dark lens and the accent (gold 8.6:1), as does every picker in light mode (gold 6.1:1). UIKit leaves a tapped toolbar control's selected label in its pressed tint, so once the press lens settles the control is re-added to its window, restoring the accent and keeping its frame. Dropping UIKit's one-point bottom alignment inset, which the controls draw over, makes Agents/Usage match the capsule beside it in height.
- **Chat/Terminal.** Compact and icon-only (speech bubble, terminal symbol, one SF Symbol language) at the right of the session header, with 44-point segments and VoiceOver labels Chat and Terminal. No glass backing: extra glass beside other glass left keyboard animations unfinished after a picker closed.
- **Agents/Usage paging.** Native interactive page scrolling reveals Usage progressively during a drag rather than switching on release; selector-triggered transitions respect Reduce Motion.
- **Dictation waveform.** An accent-colored translucent gradient, solid with Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast, fills the capsule width with recent microphone levels (silence and repeats included) on a more sensitive scale; its history clears when dictation stops or switches language.

Reference: [Apple's Liquid Glass guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views).
